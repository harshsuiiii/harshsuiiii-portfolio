<Section n="01" title="Overview">

This project explores diffusion-based generative modelling for simulating low-level CMS detector data. It was built as an ML4SCI evaluation task for Google Summer of Code. A denoising diffusion model (DDPM) is trained on unlabelled CMS jet images and compared against two baselines — a GAN and a VAE — using statistical tests.

</Section>

<Section n="02" title="Problem statement">

High-energy physics experiments depend on detector simulations that are computationally expensive. A generative model that can produce realistic detector responses is a cheaper alternative — if it gets the distribution right.

The data makes that hard. It is extremely sparse (most pixels are near zero), energy is concentrated in localised clusters, and the distribution is heavy-tailed, with rare high-energy events that matter physically.

</Section>

<Section n="03" title="Technical approach">

Train three model families on the same subset and judge them on the same measures:

- **Diffusion** — learns to reconstruct data from noise through iterative denoising, trained with an MSE loss on the predicted noise.
- **GAN** — generator against discriminator.
- **VAE** — a latent-variable model with KL regularisation.

Then compare generated and real samples with Wasserstein distance, a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, MMD, and a diversity measure across samples.

</Section>

<Section n="05" title="Implementation details">

The source images are 125 × 125 with 8 detector channels. For training they are downscaled to 32 × 32 × 8 and normalised to the range -1 to 1, using a 20,000-sample subset. The diffusion model uses 50 timesteps with a noise schedule running from 0.0001 to 0.02. Experiments are driven by one YAML configuration, and metrics, tables, plots and samples are written to a `results/` directory. The full dataset is about 38 GB and is not included in the repository.

</Section>

<Section n="08" title="Engineering challenges">

- **Metrics that lie.** On sparse data, the collapsed GAN scores the *lowest* Wasserstein distance, because near-constant, mostly empty images sit close to the mostly empty real ones. A diversity measure was needed to expose the collapse.
- **Mode collapse.** The GAN's outputs became near-constant.
- **Posterior collapse.** The VAE's KL term went to zero and its samples were over-smoothed.
- **Compute.** The configuration trains on CPU with small batches, which constrained resolution and schedule length.

</Section>
