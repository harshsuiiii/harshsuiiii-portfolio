<Section n="01" title="Overview">

An ML4SCI Google Summer of Code evaluation task: build and benchmark sparse neural network architectures for binary classification of CMS calorimeter jet data, and analyse the efficiency–accuracy trade-off under progressive weight pruning.

</Section>

<Section n="02" title="Problem statement">

Calorimeter data is extremely sparse — most detector cells record zero energy. A standard dense CNN spends most of its computation on empty space. Two questions follow:

- Does a sparsity-aware architecture outperform a standard dense CNN on this data?
- How does global L1 pruning affect accuracy as weight sparsity rises and effective FLOPs fall?

</Section>

<Section n="03" title="Technical approach">

Build a dense baseline and a sparsity-aware network, train both under the same setup, then prune the better one step by step and measure what is lost.

The key idea in SparseResNet is the sparse residual block: it computes a binary mask from the input and zeroes activations at empty detector cells after each residual computation, so the network does not propagate signal through physically meaningless regions.

</Section>

<Section n="05" title="Implementation details">

Input samples are 125 × 125 grids with 8 detector channels, max-normalised per sample to keep relative energy structure. The 10,000 samples are split 70 / 15 / 15. Both models are trained for 10 epochs with Adam at a learning rate of 1e-3, binary cross-entropy with logits, batch size 32, a reduce-on-plateau scheduler and a fixed seed.

Pruning is global and L1-unstructured at ratios from 0.2 to 0.8, with two epochs of fine-tuning after each step.

<Section n="08" title="Engineering challenges">

- **A fair comparison.** SparseResNet has about 13 times the parameters of the dense baseline, so its accuracy gain cannot be attributed to masking alone. The pruning study is what speaks to efficiency: most of those weights can be removed.
- **Measuring cost, not just size.** Weight sparsity is reported alongside effective FLOPs, since unstructured sparsity does not automatically mean faster inference.
- **Reproducibility.** Seeds, splits and hyperparameters are fixed in a config file, and result tables are committed.

</Section>
